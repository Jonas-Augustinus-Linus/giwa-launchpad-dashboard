2. Graduation: price continuity, depth cliff, overhang

2.1 Continuity (checked)

sqrtPriceX96 = sqrt(tokensFull / R) (same as Pons's PonsV2GraduationMath.sqrtPriceX96FromAmounts) with tokensFull = X_res·R/(V+R) gives tokensFull/R = X_res/(V+R) = 1/p*. So the pool opens at exactly the curve's last marginal price — the ratio is 1 by algebra, the only residual is wei-level dust (2 wei of R move the 21st digit). Fees are symmetric across the boundary (1 % on the ETH leg in both venues), sells carry no surcharge in either venue, and the crossing buyer's refund cannot be recycled cheaply (the pool's t = 0 fee is 99 %). There is no arbitrage window at graduation. The last curve buyer's average fill is far below p* (the worked example's 3.19 ETH crossing buy averages 9.5e-9 vs p* 2.06e-8) but that is the curve, not a gap; dumping that bag into the fresh pool returns 2.60 ETH for a 3.19 ETH cost.

2.2 Depth discontinuity (real, quantified)

Effective liquidity: curve L_curve = √((V+Q)·X_res) = 4.10e22; full-range pool L_pool = √(Q·tokensFull) = 2.93e22 = 0.714·L_curve (both reserves scale by Q/(V+Q)). Consequences for holders, sell side:

sell size (tokens worth … at spot) on the curve, last block in the pool, next block change
1 ETH 0.855 ETH 0.808 ETH −5.5 %
5 ETH 2.702 ETH 2.283 ETH −15.5 %
10 ETH 3.703 ETH 2.958 ETH −20.1 %

Pons has exactly this cliff; nobody advertises it. Not fixable without changing the seed ratio (a virtual-quote curve always hands over s of its virtual depth). Disclose it in the UI ("graduated pools are thinner than the curve; expect ~1.4× the price impact").

2.3 The overhang (D1 D11) — numbers

D1 puts the 8.16 % into a token-only position [MIN_TICK, floor(tick/200)·200], i.e. an ask wall starting 1.96 % above the opening price and extending to infinity. Liquidity L_over = tokensOver/√P_u = 1.18e22 = 0.289·L_curve; L_pool + L_over = 1.003·L_curve. So D1's overhang exactly restores curve-equivalent depth on the upside (a nice, non-obvious property: √(V·(V+Q)) + √(Q·…) sums back to the curve's virtual L), while the downside keeps 0.714×.

price multiple overhang sold ETH captured by overhang ETH in full-range overhang adds mcap
2× 23.9M 0.71 ETH 5.9 ETH +12 % $102k
4× 40.8M 1.71 ETH 8.4 ETH +20 % $204k
10× 55.8M 3.71 ETH 13.3 ETH +28 % $510k
100× 73.5M 15.4 ETH 42.0 ETH +37 % $5.1M

Price impact of buys right after graduation: 1 ETH → ×1.53 with the full-range position only, ×1.37 with the overhang (beyond the 2 % gap); 5 ETH → ×4.75 vs ×3.39. So the overhang dampens early pumps by ~30 % in price terms and converts into permanently locked ETH as the token rises. Three ways to read it:

  • Holder economics: strictly better (more ETH depth forever, less reflexive blow-off).
  • Promoter economics: worse (first-wave pump velocity is the marketing; the "8 % sell wall" will be pointed at in Telegram).
  • Scanner optics: pool holds 28.57 % — no different from Pons's locker holding 28.57 %; GMGN attributes both to a contract.

Versus burn (D3-8): supply 918.4M, every holder's share +8.9 %, no ask wall, simplest to explain, breaks the "exactly 1e9" line. Versus Pons's idle lock: the worst of both (dead supply that neither adds depth nor is burned).

Recommendation: keep D1's position (economically strongest) but decide now — D1 and D3 currently contradict each other (D1 locks the overhang in the pool; D3-8 burns by default and its IGraduationTarget.graduate signature assumes the leftover is already burned). This is a must-change (§12-1). If the owner prefers the pump-friendly optics, burn; do not park.

Minor: with tickSpacing = 200 the ask wall starts up to 2 % above spot; tickSpacing = 10 would close the gap to 0.1 % but breaks the "same shape as Pons" indexer story. Cosmetic; keep 200.

3. Fee level and split — competitiveness

3.1 Creator income per $1M of volume (flat, all phases)

Venue Total fee Creator share Creator $/1M Notes

Pons V2 1 % 70 % (35 % with buyback) $7,000 / $3,500 + creator tax ≤ 10 % on curve volume only
Bags-RH 2 % 50 % $10,000 2 % trader friction; ~no flow ($9k fees/day)
Bankr 1.75 % 38 % $6,650 partly paid in the memecoin; +15 % vest

pools.trade 0.25 % 40 % of ETH side ≈ $500

Ours is the best rate among 1 %-fee venues and second only to Bags, whose 2 % total fee is a volume tax (every bot/aggregator route prefers the 1 % pool). The 1 % total is the Robinhood Schelling point (Pons, o1, PAIR, Sentry, LetsCash base); GMGN/Maestro add their own 1 % on top, so the trader's all-in is already 2 %. Do not raise it; do not lower it either (0.5 % would halve creator income without visibly increasing volume — meme volume is narrative-driven, not fee-elastic at this level).

For the owner's own launches, creator + protocol = $10,000 per $1M vs $7,000 on Pons. That +$3k/$1M is the whole financial case for building the pad; break-even on a $25–60k audit is $8–20M of the owner's own-token lifetime volume (ZZZ did $27M in its first day on Pons).

3.2 80/20

Pons takes 30 %, o1 50 %, Bags 50 %, Bankr 62 %. 20 % is the most creator-favourable protocol cut on the chain; combined with 0 % team allocation it is the cleanest "creator pad" pitch available. Since the owner is both protocol and (initially) the dominant creator, the split only matters for third-party launchers; 20 % is enough to fund the only real ongoing cost (a UI). Keep.

3.3 Creation fee

0.0005 ETH ($1.24) accrued to the protocol. Note the scale effect at Pons: 26.7k launches/day × 0.0005 ETH ≈ 13.4 ETH ≈ $33k/day from launch fees alone — more than the swap-fee protocol revenue at a 1 % flow share (§8). Bot spam is revenue-positive but a feed-quality problem; the UI must rank by dev-buy size and activity, not recency. Our launch gas is ~3.5× cheaper than Pons's (≈1.1M vs 3.7M gas), which makes spam cheaper — expected, acceptable.

4. Sniper tax — shape, blocks, bots, and the creator-bot problem

4.1 Block arithmetic

Measured: 9.87 blocks/s, 10 consecutive blocks per timestamp second (launch/raw/sample_hours.log, D1 §chain facts). 3 s ≈ 30 blocks, 5 s ≈ 50. taxBps(t) = 9900 >> floor(t·14/T) has half-life T/14: 0.21 s (2 blocks) at T = 3, 0.36 s (3.5 blocks) at T = 5.

Without interpolation the Robinhood schedule is a cliff at the second boundary: ~10 blocks at 99 %, then the first block of second 1 at 6.18 % (T = 3). Bots watch block.timestamp and land exactly there. D1's arbBlockNumber interpolation is therefore not a refinement but the thing that makes the schedule exponential at all — keep it. (Its clamp min(blkMs, tsMs + 999) means the tax can only be lower than the timestamp schedule, never higher; the only failure mode — no blocks produced — errs toward higher tax.)

4.2 What bots do (EV table)

Fresh curve after a 5 % dev buy (R = 0.0884, S = 50M); a 1 ETH bot buy:

t T = 3 tax → supply → value at graduation T = 5 tax → supply → value at graduation
0.1 s 76 % → 10.8 % → 2.2 ETH (2.2×) 83 % → 7.8 % → 1.6 ETH (1.6×)
0.3 s 38 % → 24.3 % → 5.0 ETH (5.0×) 59 % → 17.6 % → 3.6 ETH (3.6×)
0.6 s 15 % → 30.7 % → 6.3 ETH (6.3×) 32 % → 26.1 % → 5.4 ETH (5.4×)
1.0 s 4 % → 33.2 % → 6.8 ETH (6.8×) 15 % → 30.7 % → 6.3 ETH (6.3×)
2.0 s 0.2 % → 34.1 % → 7.0 ETH (7.0×) 2.2 % → 33.6 % → 6.9 ETH (6.9×)

Reading: the curve is so cheap at the start (1 ETH = a third of supply) that any launch a bot believes will graduate is +EV even at 60–75 % tax. Bots therefore do not wait for 0 %; they wait until the tax is below their personal edge estimate and then race. The tax's real functions are (a) killing the pure latency race in the first ~0.3–0.5 s (where the prize would otherwise go to whoever is co-located with the sequencer), (b) skimming 5–40 % of bot entries into the fee bucket, (c) giving the disclosed dev buy a guaranteed cheapest fill. It does not give human buyers a chance: humans react at 2–5 s, by which point the tax is < 1 % on every schedule and the bots hold 30–35 % of supply. Pons's data agrees: 50 % of graduations happen within 2.2 min of launch (p10 0.8 min, p25 1.3, p75 18.6, p90 40.8; raw/grad3.log, n = 22) — the winners are decided by bots in the first minutes.

Longer windows only tax humans: o1's 20 s linear ramp charges 50 % at 10 s. Within the owner's fixed rule ("99 % → ~0 over a few seconds") the tunable is T. Recommend T = 5 s on both chains (Pons's documented value; Pons's source default is 15 s and they operate at 3 s — a choice that maximises bot volume, which is what an incumbent wants). Rationale: with interpolation, T = 5 gives 15 % at 1 s and 2 % at 2 s — bots still come, but their entry is skimmed 2–4× harder than at T = 3, and the human at 3 s still pays 0.3 %. On GIWA (1-s blocks, no interpolation) T = 5 is the only schedule whose second-1 step (24.75 %) is a meaningful skim; T = 3's 6.18 % is not. One T on both chains also keeps the Korean UI's story to one sentence.

Sub-second caveat on Robinhood: if Timeboost is enabled (D2 OQ-1, unverified), an express-lane holder gets a 200 ms head start = 2 blocks — the tax at those blocks is 76–83 %, so the express lane buys nothing profitable at launch. Fine either way.

4.3 Does the exponential give the launcher an unfair edge?

Two different edges:

  1. The disclosed dev buy (0 % tax, ≤ 5 %, same tx). Intended, visible in DevBuy, capped. Not a fairness problem — it is the market norm (Pons, o1, Bags, pump.fun all exempt the atomic first buy) and Raydium's docs call not doing it a mistake.
  2. The creator running a bot with snipe proceeds flowing 80 % back to the creator. At t = 0.3 s (T = 3) a stranger's bot pays 38.2 %; the creator's bot pays 38.2 % and gets 30.6 % back → 7.8 % effective. At t = 0.1 s: 15.5 % vs 76 %. This is undetectable (it looks like ordinary sniping), and it means the "≤ 5 % dev buy" is a floor on creator inventory, not a ceiling — the creator is structurally the best-placed sniper of their own launch. Pons has the same flaw (70 % rebate). o1 routes the surcharge to the platform, Virtuals to a vested team buyback, Clanker's sniper auction 80/20 to reward recipients.

For the owner's own launches this is moot (creator + protocol = the owner). For third-party launchers on the pad it is the single largest integrity gap in the design. Options, one escrow constant each (D2 OQ-2):

Snipe proceeds → creator-bot effective cost at t = 0.3 s (T=3) marketing verdict
80/20 bucket (draft) 7.8 % "anti-snipe tax is paid into your fee pool" (Pons parity) keeps the exploit
100 % protocol (o1) 38.2 % "the creator cannot profit from sniping their own launch" recommended
50/50 19.1 % middle acceptable
burn / bid-side ETH position at graduation 38.2 % "sniper money becomes permanent liquidity" best optics, more code (third position); v1.1

Recommendation: 100 % protocol in v1 (one constant), and say why on the launch page. Cost to a third-party creator: on a bot-heavy launch the snipe skim is a few % of the first ~2 ETH of buys — tens of dollars — vs a trust feature the pad can advertise that Pons cannot.

4.4 Post-graduation snipe (D2 §4)

Graduation is not a price discontinuity (§2.1), so the "prize" is only the marketing surge after the ipop/DexScreener alert. Pons's hook has no post-graduation tax (verified: no snipe symbol in PonsV2MemeHook.sol); D2 adds a 3 s staircase [98, 24, 2] %. Keep it — it costs nothing when nobody snipes and it protects the graduation second (which is inside someone else's buy tx, so a human cannot be in it by accident unless their router falls back from curve to pool inside the same second — the UI must not do that). But use the same schedule function as the curve (one library, one T) instead of a second table; today D1 (exponential, 14 halvings, T = 3) and D2 ([9800, 2400, 200], integer seconds) disagree, and D3 describes a third ("98 % → 0 over SNIPE_SECONDS"). Must-change §12-4.

4.5 Optional, inside the rules: per-tx size cap during the window

Not a tax, not an exempt list: "while t &lt; T, one buy may take at most 10 % of the remaining sellable supply". Multi-wallet bots defeat it, but on an FCFS chain that means 3–4 competing bots each get a slice instead of one bot taking 34 % — better holder distribution for the same bot volume. Pons V1 hard-coded 5 % per wallet in blocks 1–2. Worth a v1.1 look; not required.

Observed Pons first buys (34/40 same-block): median 1.16 %, p75 2.86 %, p90 6.86 %, max 20.96 %. A 5 % cap admits ~85 % of observed launcher behaviour and refuses the p90+ tail that scanners flag. Is 5 % enough for the launcher "to matter"? For an experienced launcher the dev buy is a signal ("skin in the game, not a bot"), not the inventory — inventory comes from the fee stream ($8k–10k per $1M) and, honestly, from buying at t ≥ 1 s like everyone else (§4.3). 6. Fee-0 pool, third-party liquidity, depth

  • Why nobody LPs: with LP fee 0 and the hook taking 1 % in quote outside the position, a third-party LP earns 0 and carries impermanent loss. They will not come; JIT is pointless. Depth is exactly the graduation liquidity: 4.2 ETH + 204.08M (+ overhang), growing as Q·√m in ETH terms (5.9 ETH at 2×, 13.3 at 10×, 42 at 100×). Ratio of ETH depth to mcap: 20 % at graduation, 6.5 % at 10×, 2 % at 100× — same as pump.fun/Pons. Fine for a memecoin; the alternative (deeper pools) only exists if someone donates capital.
  • Why not a small LP tier (e.g. 0.3 % LP + 1 % hook): the locked position would earn 20–28 % of that LP fee (its share of in-range liquidity) with no collect path → permanently stranded in the PoolManager, i.e. burned value; third-party LPs would earn the rest and compete with the creator for the same trade. Only sensible if the locker had a collect-and-forward path — which D3 rightly refuses (it is the attack surface). Keep fee 0.
  • The real cost of fee 0 — parallel pools: for big tokens, LPs create their own pool (hookless v4 1 %, v3 1 %, USDG pairs) to earn fees; routers split by depth; the creator loses that share. Observed on Robinhood: NEST 30 v4 pairs, VIBES 1 v3 + 4 v4, FATCOIN's largest volume in an EOA-made USDG pool (01 §3). Unpreventable on v4 (any key can be initialised) and not gated by the token (no transfer hooks). Mitigations already in the design (100 % of graduation liquidity in the hooked pool, canonical PoolRegistered id, UI routing) are the only ones available. Expected leakage: 0 for the 97 % of tokens that never matter; meaningful (tens of %) only on the handful that reach $10M+ mcap — i.e. exactly where the creator income is. Accept and disclose.
  • Uniswap protocol fee (≤ 0.1 % per direction, governance-controlled) can appear on top; traders pay it, creators unaffected. Disclose.

7. Creator tax ≤ 2 % — drop

Curve volume for a graduating token is a few × the raise (4.2 ETH + churn; call it 10–20 ETH ≈ $25–50k); a 2 % tax on that is $500–1,000 once, versus $8,000 per $1M of post-graduation volume. The tax is only material on tokens that never graduate — 97.4 % of launches — where it extracts more from the buyers of a failed launch. That is the reputational shape of the feature ("read the tax before trading" is boilerplate on Pons's docs; GMGN/GoPlus label tax tokens). Pons's sample of 22 graduates: 13 used tax 0, 3 used 1 %, 2 used 1.5 %, 2 used 2 %, one each 8 % and 10 % — creators who graduate mostly do not use it.

Costs of keeping it: one immutable per launch, a code path in the curve and (per D2 OQ-3) possibly the hook and pair, a register() argument, an extra fee bucket in the escrow, a Korean-UI explainer, and a scanner label. Benefit: parity with a Pons feature the good launches do not use.

Recommendation: drop in v1 (owner's rule says "optional", so optional-off is inside the rule). If kept: curve-only (never in the hook/pair), ≤ 200 bps, default 0, immutable, shown on the token card as "creator tax 2 % on the curve" in red. Either way, resolve D2 OQ-3 as curve-only.

8. Protocol revenue scenarios and the shape of the distribution

Flow proxies (DefiLlama pons-v2, launch/sum_pons-v2.json): 24 h $8.75M (09-04 peak), 7 d avg $5.33M/day, 30 d avg $1.58M/day; the series went $139k (08-16) → $1.2M (08-26) → $4.7M (08-30) → $8.75M (09-04). DefiLlama's Pons figure includes launch fees, curve fees, creator taxes, and swept hook fees; a bottom-up cross-check (1 % of curve volume $161M + top-20 graduated pools $198M) gives ≈ $3.6M/day, so the $8.75M contains a large creator-tax and/or long-tail-pool component. Treat $3.6–5.3M/day as "current Robinhood launch-flow fees".

Plus launch fees: at 1 % of Pons's launch count (267/day) ≈ 0.13 ETH ≈ $330/day.

Distribution. GeckoTerminal's top-20 Pons graduated pools by 24 h volume: #1 (SLINK/USDG) $66M = 34 % of the top-20's $198M; top-3 53 %; top-5 63 %; top-10 82 %. The top-20 are ~3 % of the 2-day graduation cohort and 0.08 % of launches. Fee income is therefore a power law with α ≈ 1.1: the median launch earns ~$0, the median graduate earns a few hundred dollars over its life, and a single ZZZ-class token ($27M/day) yields $270k/day in fees ($54k/day protocol) — more than the entire "1 % share" scenario. Two consequences:

  1. "1 % of launch flow" is not a revenue plan; expected revenue at 1 % of launches is well below 1 % of fees, because winners follow the audience (GMGN feeds, DexScreener boosts, the Pons Explore tab), not the contracts. A new pad with 267 launches/day and Pons-quality flow would see a top-20-class token every ~5 days in expectation; with realistic early flow, every few weeks. Variance dominates: plan on months of $0–2k/day punctuated by five-figure days.
  2. The bankable revenue is the owner's own launches (100 % of a 1 % fee → $10k per $1M) and, on GIWA, being the only pad with a creator share at all (GIWA MARKETS pays creators 0 %, Joseon-style pads 0.3 % swap). The 20 % protocol cut on third-party launches is upside, not the base.

9. GIWA day-1 variant

D1 proposes V = 0.6, Q = 1.5 (start $1.5k, graduation $18k, same 12.25×) as a permanent immutable because ETH is scarce on day 1. Issues:

  • Permanence: within weeks GIWA will have bridged ETH and CEX rails (KR funding via CEX → Arbitrum → Relay is already the owner's plan), and a 1.5 ETH bar ($3.7k) then means pools that are rug-thin (a 0.5 ETH buy moves price +71 %). Changing Q requires a new curve and a new factory (the factory is an immutable of the curve and vice-versa), and a new factory changes the CREATE3 namespace of every future token — the same-address-on-both-chains property is lost for all launches after the switch unless Robinhood also redeploys. That is an expensive way to turn one number.
  • Self-graduation at Q = 1.5 costs the creator only fees (≈ 1 % × 2 + snipe): they end up holding 71.4 % of supply plus a "graduated" badge with $3.7k of locked ETH. Cheap badge; Pons's "graduation is not a quality signal" boilerplate becomes more true the lower Q is.
  • ETH-only quote: correct on GIWA (nothing else exists day 1) and correct for v1 on Robinhood for the reasons D1 gives, but note the cost: on Robinhood, USDG pairs were 24 % of Pons launches and 3 of this week's 5 largest Pons tokens were non-ETH pairs (SLINK/USDG, Ponsan/USDG, OZZY/USDG, SPY/NOTHING). v1.1 should add USDG. If a canonical stablecoin never comes to GIWA, ETH remains the quote and nothing in the economics breaks — KRW is a display-layer conversion; the only casualty is the "stable-denominated graduation bar" story, which Robinhood pads have but do not need.

Recommendation — an immutable tier table, selectable per launch (no admin, no setter; the table is a constructor constant; the launcher picks tier ∈ {0, 1, 2} at createToken; the curve stores 1 byte in the spare bytes of its per-token slot):

tier V Q start FDV graduation FDV intended use
0 "seed" 0.6 1.5 $1.5k $18k GIWA day-1; small-community tests
1 "standard" 1.68 4.2 $4.2k $51k Robinhood default; GIWA once ETH is available
2 "deep" 2.8 7.0 $6.9k $85k serious launches wanting Bags-class depth

All tiers keep Q = 2.5·V so the percentages (71.43 / 20.41 / 8.16), the 12.25× multiple, the dev-buy cap in tokens and the seed-liquidity formula are identical — only the ETH scale changes. The UI defaults to tier 1 on Robinhood and tier 0 on GIWA and can move the GIWA default with a UI change, not a redeploy. Token address parity across chains is unaffected (the tier is not in the salt). Cost: one immutable array, one byte of state, a tier argument, one more fuzz dimension.

If a single immutable per chain is kept anyway: GIWA Q = 1.5 is defensible for a Sepolia rehearsal, but for the 9134 mainnet deployment wait for observed bridge liquidity (D1 §9 already says this) — do not ship a permanent Q = 1.5.

12. Must-change (before audit freeze)

  1. Overhang disposition is contradictory across D1 and D3. D1 D11 locks the 8.16 % as a second position; D3-8 burns it by default and IGraduationTarget.graduate assumes the leftover is already burned. Pick one (recommendation: D1's position, §2.3), and make D3's target mint both positions (or burn) accordingly. A compile-time constant is fine, but the two documents must agree before the executor/target interface is frozen.
  2. Curve-phase fees and the creator recipient live in two places. D1 accrues curve fees in the BondingCurve singleton with its own claimCreatorFees and keeps the recipient + 3-day timelock in the factory; D2/D3 keep the ledger, recipient and timelock in FeeEscrow. Two recipients and two timelocks for one token is an economic bug (a creator could point curve fees and pool fees at different wallets; the DefiLlama adapter over FeesAccrued would miss all curve-phase fees; the escrow's FACTORY.register and the curve's factory.creatorRecipient can diverge). Unify: the curve credits FeeEscrow.credit{value}(token, …) per trade (or, to save the external call, accrues and lets anyone flush(token) into the escrow), the escrow is the only ledger and the only recipient registry, the factory holds no recipient state.
  3. D1 §5.5 lists the hook flags of an afterSwap-only hook (BEFORE_INITIALIZE | AFTER_SWAP | AFTER_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA), which is Pons's design that D2 rejects because the fee would land in the memecoin on exact-in buys and need an operator to convert. D1 must reference D2's 0x22CC (or 0x20CC) and its register()-before-initialize ordering; otherwise component 1's executor and component 2's hook cannot be wired.
  4. One snipe schedule. Three different functions today (D1 exponential with 14 halvings and sub-second interpolation; D2 table [9800, 2400, 200] by integer second; D3 "98 % → 0 over SNIPE_SECONDS"). Ship one pure library snipeBps(elapsedMs, T) used by curve, hook and pair, with T a single constant (recommend 5 s), interpolation on Robinhood only. Divergent schedules are auditable surprises and a UI that cannot state one rule.
  5. Curve tier table instead of a single immutable (V, Q) per chain (§9). An immutable Q = 1.5 on GIWA will be wrong within weeks of mainnet, and fixing it costs the cross-chain address namespace.
  6. Decide snipe-proceeds routing explicitly (§4.3) and document it. The draft's silence (D2 OQ-2 open, D1 "joins the 80/20 bucket") leaves the largest third-party-creator integrity gap undecided. Recommendation: 100 % protocol.
  7. Creator tax: decide drop/keep, and if kept, curve-only (§7; resolves D2 OQ-3 and D1 §8 Q3). The hook and pair should not carry a tax path in v1.

Not economics, but noticed while reading and worth a line to the other reviewers: D1 has the curve credit ETH dust to CURVE.creditProtocolFees, D3 to escrow.credit(token) — same unification as item 2; D3's IGraduationTarget is onlyFactory while D1's executor is onlyCurve — pick one caller.

13. Accepted risks (economic)

  • Depth cliff at graduation (−5 % to −20 % realised proceeds on large sells vs the last curve block). Structural to any virtual-quote curve; same on Pons; disclose.
  • Sniper tax does not protect humans; it prices the latency race and skims bots. Bots take ~30 % of supply of any launch they believe in, within 1–2 s, on every schedule inside the owner's "few seconds" rule.
  • Parallel fee-earning pools on big tokens leak volume and fees from the creator's hooked pool; unpreventable on v4; bounded by routing share.
  • Revenue is a power law: third-party protocol revenue is a lottery on 0.1 % of launches; base revenue is the owner's own launches and launch fees.
  • Bot-spam launches are cheaper here (≈ 1.1M gas) than on Pons; revenue-positive, feed-noise-negative.
  • Overhang ask wall (if D1's position is kept) dampens early pumps by ~30 % in price terms; a marketing negative traded for permanent ETH depth.
  • ETH-only quote in v1 forgoes the USDG/stock-pair segment that produced 3 of Pons's top-5 tokens this week; v1.1 item.
  • Uniswap governance protocol fee (≤ 0.1 %/direction) may be switched on; traders pay, creators unaffected.
  • Timestamp granularity on GIWA (1 s, fee-ordered): the second-1 block is a priority-fee auction whose proceeds go to the sequencer; the tax skim is the only part the pad captures.
